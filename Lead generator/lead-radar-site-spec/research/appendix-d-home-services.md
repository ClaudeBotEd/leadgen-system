# Appendix D — Home-Services Lead Marketplaces and Contractor Ops

Two categories: (A) consumer-facing lead marketplaces — Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, Bark, Werkspot — and (B) operational SaaS for trades — ServiceTitan, Jobber.

---

## 1. Half A: Consumer-facing lead marketplaces

### 1.1 Angi / HomeAdvisor

**A. Positioning.** Consumer hero: *"See highly-rated home maintenance professionals for free. Read real local reviews and grades from neighbors so you can pick the right pro for the job the first time."*

Pro side (`signup.angi.com/pro`):

> **"Grow your business on the largest homeowner network"**
> *"Over 1.5M opportunities on Angi last month; get started to start receiving leads."*

Page title: *"Get Leads, Win Jobs, Grow Your Business | Angi Pro"* — telegraphs the three-noun marketplace formula. Asymmetry: harder to consumers, softer to pros.

**B. Visual language.** Green-fading-to-yellow heritage palette. White space, photographic homeowner portraits, category cards. Pro page: five generic SVG panels behind a service dropdown.

**C. Lead quality to pros.** "1.5M opportunities last month" — volume, not quality. "Opportunity" used *instead of* "lead" — softer term post-FTC settlement.

**D. Trust mechanics for pros.** Almost none publicly. **Lead Credit Guidelines** (`pro.homeadvisor.com/terms/lead-credit-guidelines/`):

> *"Not eligible for credits: Consumer is not calling you back. Consumer already found someone to complete the work. Consumer changed their mind. Consumer is just 'shopping.' You did not win the job."*

Refund window 30 days; issued credits expire in 6 months. **FTC fined HomeAdvisor up to $7.2M in January 2023** for *"making false, misleading, or unsubstantiated claims about the quality and source of the leads"* — including misrepresenting "intent to hire" since mid-2014.

**E. Operational UX in marketing.** None. Pros funneled to phone-sales (833-777-0260).

**F. Pricing.** Hidden. Industry reporting: ~$288–$300 annual fee + $15–$120 per lead.

---

### 1.2 Thumbtack

**A. Positioning.** Consumer: *"Find the perfect professional for you. Get free quotes within minutes."*

Pro hero (`thumbtack.com/pro`):

> *"Hear from customers who choose you, with high intent to hire."*
> *"Over 30,000+ leads on Thumbtack per day — no charge to join, no annual fees, and no membership fees."*
> *"We limit competition for each job among pros. We match you with jobs that exactly fit your preferences."*
> *"Powerful tools help you budget and control how many leads you get."*

Three implicit reassurances: *"high intent to hire"* (quality framing — same language FTC found deceptive at HomeAdvisor), *"limit competition"* (exclusivity-adjacent), *"budget and control"* (agency framing).

**B. Visual language.** Cleanest of the consumer marketplaces. White background, soft rounded cards, blue/teal CTA. Pro page denser — dashboard glimpses, lead-card mockups, budgeting UI.

**C. Lead quality.** Per-lead costs $15–$80+. Refund policy difficult per contractor reviews.

**D. Trust mechanics.** "30,000+ leads per day."

**E. Operational UX.** Strongest of marketplaces. Pro app and Dashboard screenshots featured.

**F. Pricing.** Pay-per-lead. No subscription. Variable.

---

### 1.3 Bark

**A. Positioning.** Consumer: *"Find the perfect professional for you. Get free quotes within minutes."* Same as Thumbtack — category template.

Seller hero (`bark.com/en/us/how-it-works/sellers/`):

> *"Bark is the Amazon of services. Millions of people use us worldwide to find what they need every day."*
> *"From the moment you sign up, we'll send you leads for free. You only pay to contact customers that you think are the right fit for your business."*
> *"Bark professionals receive hot, live leads as soon as they are placed."*

*"The Amazon of services"* — most cynically commodity self-description. They tell pros they are inventory.

**B. Visual language.** Navy blue, aspirational lifestyle across 100+ categories.

**C. Lead quality framing.** *"hot, live leads."* Pricing page:

> *"We use a simple credit system. Buy a credit pack and simply use the credits to contact the customers you're interested in. No commission, no hidden fees."*
> *"You decide which leads you respond to and you'll know the cost in credits upfront."*
> *"We calculate the costs of contacting a customer based on the service, the value of the job, and the supply and demand in the area."*

Most transparent pricing copy in marketplace category. Credits ~US$2.35 each.

**D. Trust mechanics.** The guarantee:

> *"We're so confident you'll win business with your first credit pack, we'll return all your credits if you don't. No questions asked."*

Reality: credits returned in *more credits*, not money. As of November 1, 2025, "all Credits are valid for 3 months from the date of purchase."

**E. Operational UX.** Minimal.

**F. Pricing.** Pure credit pre-pay.

### 1.4 Werkspot — direct competitor (werkspot.nl)

**A. Positioning.** Consumer hero:

> **"Werkspot — De betrouwbare manier om vakmensen in te huren"**
> *"Plaats je klus gratis op Werkspot en ontvang voorstellen van vakmannen bij jou in de buurt."*
> *"Heb je een klus in of om het huis liggen? Laat het over aan een echte vakman."*

Owned by **Instapro Group** (pan-European Bark/Thumbtack analog).

Vakman pitch (from klusvisie.nl partner copy quoting Werkspot's three-promise stack):

> **"Gratis toegang tot mooi werk voor vakmannen"**
> ✓ *"Gratis toegang tot mooi werk"*
> ✓ *"Vul je agenda"*
> ✓ *"Ontvang online reviews"*
> *"Als vakman kies je zelf welke opdracht bij jou past."*
> *"Je betaalt pas wanneer de opdrachtgever bij interesse zijn of haar telefoonnummer met je deelt."*

Asymmetry: consumer-side promises *quality* (echte vakman, betrouwbare manier), pro-side promises *volume* (vul je agenda) + *autonomy* (kies zelf).

**B. Visual language.** White backgrounds, photographic homeowner imagery, green/orange accent (orange dominant on CTAs), rounded service-category cards, humanist sans-serif. Trust: "268.000 reviews," service-area selector. Visually conservative.

**C. Lead quality (smoking gun).** Werkspot's framing:

> *"Via reviews die consumenten schrijven over het uitgevoerde werk, ontstaat een goed beeld van de kwaliteit van de vakman."*
> *"de klus dan nog niet binnen, maar de kans daarop is zeer groot"*

The phrase *"de kans daarop is zeer groot"* is the **Dutch equivalent of HomeAdvisor's "intent to hire"** — the soft promise FTC fined HomeAdvisor for. Werkspot tells installers leads are *very likely* to convert while charging €15–€45 per lead (up to €75 for large jobs).

**D. Trust mechanics for pros.** Platform stats: **360,000 klussen/year, 11,000 vakmensen, 268,000 reviews**. No published refund policy on marketing surface.

**E. Operational UX.** Almost none visible.

**F. Pricing.** Pay-per-lead. €3–€75 per lead, typically €15–€45.

### 1.5 Why installers hate Werkspot (primary Dutch sources)

From wandgrimeurs.nl ("Waarheid over Werkspot"):

> *"Aan de buitenkant proberen de kluswebsites zoals Werkspot de mensen/klanten aan de vraagzijde op elk mogelijke manier te lokken onder het mom van 'extra kwaliteitseisen, juiste vakmannen' etc. Maar aan de binnenkant, de aanbodzijde, bevinden zich hoofdzakelijk nog maar 'contactgestoorde en kwalitatief ongeschikte vaklieden.'"*

> *"Door hun eigen klachten, maar ook gevoed door klachten van andere vakmannen over Werkspot, komen ze langzaam tot het besef dat ze worden uitgewrongen, uitgemolken en misschien wel compleet afhankelijk zijn geworden."*

> *"De hoge tarieven en het immorele verdienmodel pakken de laatste toppers die er zijn één voor één hun biezen."*

Consumer side gets quality-theater; pro side gets exploitation. Pros realize they are *uitgewrongen en uitgemolken* (wrung out and milked dry).

## 2. Half B: Operational SaaS for the trades

### 2.1 ServiceTitan

**A. Positioning.** *"The #1 software for commercial and residential trades."* / *"All-in-one software built to accelerate today's contracting businesses."* / *"Build your legacy with ServiceTitan."*

**Single-audience product.** No consumer side. Pro is the customer.

**B. Visual language.** Corporate blue (#026FDC), commercial photography of HVAC techs and trucks, clean UI mockups, customer logos. Enterprise B2B SaaS register.

**C. Value to contractors — operating ratios**:
- *"+9% in revenue per technician"*
- *"+21% in Service Agreement renewals"*
- *"+16% increase in annual revenue"*
- *"+10% Invoices paid on time"*

Every claim a *delta to the contractor's existing business* — not promise of new customers.

**D. Trust mechanics — named testimonials with revenue numbers:**

> *"ServiceTitan is the best solution. We went from doing $16 million to this year doing just under $20 million."* — Gary Cosmer, Lovett Services
> *"I couldn't have grown 87% last year without ServiceTitan."* — Tom Howard, Lee's Air
> *"ServiceTitan helped grow my sales by 70%. We're on pace to surpass $52M."* — John Akhoian, Rooter Hero

Anchor: *"100,000+ service professionals trust ServiceTitan to run their business."*

**E. Operational UX.** Rich. Proposal Builder, Job Costing, Service Agreements, Client Portal — named features with screen previews.

**F. Pricing.** Not published.

---

### 2.2 Jobber

**A. Positioning.** *"Run a stronger service business."* / *"From first quote to final payment, Jobber keeps your work, team, and revenue moving forward."* / *"The all-in-one solution for home service pros."*

Same single-audience contractor focus. Lighter, friendlier, SMB-feel.

**B. Visual language.** Field-photography of crews in action, mid-density product mocks, warmer blues and oranges. Friendly SMB SaaS — closer to QuickBooks or Squarespace.

**C. Value communication.** *"29 million+ Jobs completed,"* *"12 hours+ Saved per week,"* *"44% Revenue growth on average in first year,"* *"50+ Field service industries,"* *"100K+ Businesses,"* *"400,000+ service professionals."*

Every stat about contractor outcomes.

**D. Trust mechanics.** Named field-pro testimonial:
> *"I went from 20 customers to now 60 customers."* — Stacy Flanagan, SEF The Lawn Surgeon

Ratings: 4.8 (13,861 reviews) App Store, 4.5 (4,883) Google Play.

**E. Operational UX.** Extremely rich. Four pillars — *Get Noticed, Win Jobs, Work Smarter, Boost Profits*. Marketing Suite ($79/mo) and Jobber AI Receptionist ($99/mo) add-ons publicly priced.

**F. Pricing.** Tiered, transparent.

---

### 2.3 What ServiceTitan/Jobber signal that marketplaces can't

1. **Single-audience focus.** No two-sided asymmetry.
2. **Outcome metrics as deltas.** "+9% revenue per technician" / "44% revenue growth in first year."
3. **Named testimonials with verified revenue.** Marketplaces structurally can't — contractors won't publicly endorse Angi.
4. **Product screens as marketing.** Dashboard IS the pitch.
5. **Tiered or sales-gated pricing — not pay-per-lead.** Revenue aligned with customer success.

## Synthesis (500 words)

**The marketplace archetype is structurally a commodity classifier.** Across Angi, Thumbtack, Bark, Werkspot, consumer-facing surface promises *quality through review*, pro-facing surface promises *volume through opportunity*. Visual language interchangeable. Pricing: pay-per-lead (Thumbtack, Werkspot, Angi) or pre-pay credits (Bark). What feels commodity is the implicit promise to the wrong side — pros sold a number (1.5M opportunities, 30,000 leads/day, 360,000 klussen/year), then asked to swim against shared-lead competition for a job they paid to bid on. The FTC settlement against HomeAdvisor proves this isn't stylistic: *"false, misleading, or unsubstantiated claims about the quality and source of the leads"* (FTC.gov, January 2023).

**The operational-SaaS archetype is the inverse.** ServiceTitan and Jobber sell *operating leverage on the contractor's existing book of business* — revenue per technician, service-agreement renewals, hours saved, invoice cash flow. They show dashboards because dashboards are the product. They use named testimonials with revenue deltas because customers will publicly endorse them.

**Dominant "lead quality" claims that have lost meaning:** *"verified," "qualified," "high intent," "ready-to-buy," "hot leads," "exclusive,"* and any sentence containing *"de kans daarop is zeer groot"*. All used by HomeAdvisor, fined by FTC. None credible in 2026.

**What Lead Radar must avoid to escape "another Werkspot" mental bucket:** the three-noun marketplace formula ("Get Leads, Win Jobs, Grow Your Business"), volume-stat hero ("X million opportunities"), lifestyle homeowner photography, credit-pack mechanics, "free to join, pay-per-lead" copy, category-tile grids resembling Bark, any promise framed around the pro choosing from a queue of strangers. Crucially: avoid *vakmensen, klussen, offertes* in the hero — those words *are* Werkspot's positioning.

**What Lead Radar could credibly own that no marketplace claims:** *evidence per lead* (transparent signal trail showing *why* this is the right buyer, surfaced before contact); *no-shared-distribution* (the lead is yours alone — and you can see it is); *no charge until a real meeting* (skin in the game on Lead Radar's side); *intent receipts* (verifiable purchase-stage signals, not "high intent" rhetoric). The operational frame — "the inbox of warm buyers your sales team already wishes they had" — is unclaimed.

**The opportunity gap.** Between consumer marketplace and operations SaaS sits unoccupied position: a **lead-intelligence layer** that *behaves like Jobber-grade software* on the installer side (single-audience, dashboard-first, outcome-metric framed) but *replaces the marketplace* upstream. Category needs a product where the pro is unambiguously the customer, the lead is unambiguously theirs alone, and pricing aligns with their P&L. That's the unclaimed seat.
