## 1. Common Room — commonroom.io

### A. Positioning & promise (verbatim)

**Hero:** "The AI GTM Platform"
**Subhead:** "Complete buyer intelligence. Real pipeline impact."
**Primary CTA:** "Request a demo"
**Self-described category:** "The AI GTM Platform" — also "Revenue agents built on complete buyer intelligence"

Reinforcing copy: *"The winners won't have better AI. They'll know their buyers better."* The phrase *"Stop stitching together. Start selling."* runs as sub-headline. Closing CTA: *"Stop guessing. Start knowing."*

### B. Visual language

Light-mode, neutral base, blue accent. No gradients. Sans-serif weight contrast for hierarchy. Generous whitespace. No decorative charts — strongest visual asset is **the customer logo wall** (Squarespace, Zendesk, Workato, Anthropic, Otter, Splunk, Atlassian, Asana, dbt Labs, Airbyte, Notion, Replit, Superhuman, Weaviate). Verbatim trust phrase: *"Used by world-class GTM teams."*

### C. How they visualize "intent" / "signals" — CRITICAL

No single "intent score" as hero metaphor. **Signal categories as named modules**:
- "Job changes" (`/product/job-change/`)
- "Website visits" (`/product/web-visitor-identification/`)
- "Dark funnel" (`/product/signals/dark-funnel/`)
- "Product-led sales"
- Referenced in copy: product usage, GitHub activity, CRM and call transcript data, open source activity tracking.

Hero metaphor: **"Light up the dark funnel."** Verbatim problem-frame: *"Real buying intent on social, community, open source, and other dark funnel channels is missed. When signals are captured, the person and account behind the signal are unknown."*

Product answer is agentic capture: *"Send RoomieAI™ Capture, our agent for signal capture, on a mission to seek and serve up any bespoke signals that are relevant to your GTM motion."*

They do **not** use labels "Surging," "In-Market," "Pulse," or a numeric heat score. Language is taxonomic, not scalar. Differentiator: *"Person-level. No gaps."*

### D. Information hierarchy

1. Hero + subhead + Request demo CTA
2. Customer logo wall
3. "A different approach"
4. Module cards: Complete Buyer Intelligence / AI Agents / Revenue Control Plane / Embedded Workflow Activation
5. Person360™ + RoomieAI™ feature blocks
6. "Real results from real teams" — testimonial w/ Semgrep + 74% pipeline metric
7. CTA + FAQ

### E. Trust mechanics

No SOC2/GDPR/G2 badges on homepage. Linguistic enterprise-trust: *"Enterprise-grade governance, role-based permissioning, territory management,"* *"full audit trails,"* *"configurable Revenue Control Plane."* Short pull quote + hard number (*"Buy it, please"* — 74% pipeline lift, 2.5x meetings).

### Pricing
- Essential: $2,100/mo annually, 5 seats
- Advanced: Custom, 15 seats
- Enterprise: Custom, 30 seats

### E. Trust mechanics — the cleverest in the category

RB2B *bakes* compliance into a section on the homepage: *"RB2B is compliant with all applicable laws and regulations."* Key brag: *"Person-Level ID is US-Only, GDPR doesn't apply. To enable Global Company-Level ID, consent capture is required to comply with GDPR. We're CCPA compliant."*

More granular: *"For Person-Level ID: No. Our database of personal information only contains profiles that have been matched to US home addresses, and we use IP ringfencing to only resolve US traffic."*

This is masterful: they **explain their architecture in one sentence** so the buyer instantly understands why it's legal.

Testimonials are *long verbatim wins with dollar figures*: *"Using RB2B, we identified a high-value website visitor and added them to a targeted sequence, which led to closing a $100K opportunity."*

**Type:** sans-serif with ALL-CAPS micro-labels ("CONNECT TO OVER 50 APPS"). Mixed case in CTAs ("try rb2b for free >>>") is a deliberate brand mark — reads like a Slack DM from a builder. Lowercase wordmark.

**Color:** dark navy + bright cyan-teal. Cyan used assertively on logo, CTAs, underlines.

**Tone:** anti-corporate but not sloppy. Direct, numeric, results-bound. Confident-claim register: *"The site visit is the best signal, and RB2B has the highest quality site visit in the market."* They don't hedge. Footer *"rb2b™Since2026©"* — trademarked but written like a sticker.

**Pricing approach:** self-serve, transparent, ladder-priced.

- Starter: $79/mo — 300 resolutions

Only company in category with a real free tier, sub-$100 starter, and published per-resolution ladders.

**Founder Adam Robinson:** On website, *understated*. Footer links to "Adam's Linkedin" and "Adam's YouTube"; referenced as "RetentionAdam." No founder bio in hero. The site is the *fulfillment surface*.

## Cross-cuts

### Founder-led vs enterprise
- **Founder-led:** RB2B. Operator-targeted, self-serve, $0–$199 ladder, conversational copy.
- **Founder-flavored, enterprise-sized:** Common Room. Voice (*"Stop guessing. Start knowing."*) but pricing starts $2,100/mo.
- **Enterprise:** 6sense. Demo-only. Trademarks. Logos like Shell and SAP.

### Premium intelligence vs data pipe
- **Premium intelligence ("we tell you the answer"):** 6sense, Common Room.
- **Data pipe ("we give you the signal, you decide"):** Bombora, RB2B, Dealfront.
- RB2B is a pipe by architecture but UX-promise (Slack ping with a real person) makes it *feel* like intelligence.

### Pricing transparency (most → least)
1. RB2B — full ladder, real free tier, self-serve.
2. Leadfeeder/Dealfront — 12-rung ladder €99–€1,199, free Lite.

4. 6sense — free visible, paid opaque, demo required.

### Dashboard busy-ness (most → least)

## Synthesis (≤400 words)

**What the category collectively projects.** One promise: *we see things you can't see.* Dark-funnel frame is table stakes. Common Room owns the phrase but 6sense ("only 5-10% of accounts are in-market"), Bombora ("the meaning behind the words on a webpage"), Dealfront ("ICP-match companies already visiting"), and RB2B ("identify 70-80% of your website traffic") all sell the same shape. Category trades on **epistemic asymmetry**.

**Universal patterns.** Light-mode SaaS palette + single saturated accent (teal at 6sense/RB2B, blue at Common Room, green at Dealfront, charcoal at Bombora). Sans-serif throughout. Customer logo wall within first scroll. Trademark or coined term per company (Signalverse, Company Surge, Person360, Context360, RoomieAI, Dark Funnel-as-product). 5-tile use-case grid. Testimonials with hard numbers. Stage models survive at high end (6sense's Target → Purchase) but category is moving toward **taxonomic signal types**.

**The spread — data-bro enterprise vs founder-craft.** 6sense and Bombora at data-bro pole: trademarks, opaque pricing, demo-only, trillion-signal claims. RB2B at founder-craft pole: published ladder, self-serve, conversational, lowercase CTAs, compliance in two sentences. Common Room in the middle. Dealfront on its own axis: European, GDPR-as-pillar, pragmatic.

**Emerging signal-visualization conventions.** (1) Named signal *types* replacing single intent scores. (2) Slack-notification-of-a-person becoming canonical UX artifact. (3) "Person-level" the new differentiator over "account-level." (4) AI-agent framing expected on every homepage.

**What Lead Radar would inherit by accident.** *Inherited goods:* instant credibility (category teaches buyers to expect signal feeds, stage models, ICP language), trust shortcuts via logo walls + compliance-on-homepage. *Inherited bads:* (1) teal-accent / light-SaaS look has homogenized — every competitor looks like every other; (2) trademark-everything instinct (Signalverse, Person360) reads as desperate scarcity-building; (3) demo-only opacity has trained buyers to distrust — anyone showing a published price feels honest by default; (4) "complete buyer intelligence" and "AI GTM" are already collapsing into noise. A new entrant inherits "yet another intent tool" pattern-recognition unless something on the page breaks convention deliberately.
